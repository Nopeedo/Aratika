# Politika launch video script

~2:20 · politika.nz · 24 September 2026

The first video announcement of the site. Written from Tawhiao's own recording,
reorganised so the two problems he names in section 2 are the two things
section 4 answers.

---

## The script

### 1 · Who I am

*0:00–0:25 · Straight to camera. No rush.*

> Kia ora. My name's Tawhiao. I'm a dad and a husband, and I grew up here in Hamilton.
>
> I'm thirty years old, and I've never voted. Not once.
>
> This year I decided I would.

### 2 · The problem

*0:25–1:00 · The heart of it. Slow down, you're describing something that happened to you.*

> So I started looking. And it got overwhelming.
>
> There's so much on the news and social media, and it's hard to tell the difference between what a party says and what they actually mean.
>
> I realised there were two problems. First, to find out what a party actually stands for, you've got to go to their website. Every party, one at a time. And if I'm honest, I just couldn't do it.
>
> Second, when I did get there, I couldn't really understand it.
>
> So I built something.

### 3 · What it is

*1:00–1:12 · Don't add to it.*

> It's called Politika, and it's free.

### 4 · How the comparison works

*1:12–1:45 · The core. Screen recording over this, tap through one issue slowly. Housing or cost of living will land hardest.*

> You pick an issue — housing, health, cost of living, climate, crime, immigration. There's eleven.
>
> Then you see where every party stands on that one issue, on the same page. That's the first problem sorted — you're not going to eleven websites.
>
> And it's written in normal words. You can read the short version or the full version.
>
> If we don't have a party's position on something yet, the page says so.

### 5 · The rest of it

*1:45–1:55 · Light touch. Screen recording keeps moving.*

> There's other tools in there too, to help you feel sure about your vote. Have a look around.

### 6 · Where it's at

*1:55–2:10 · Back to camera. Say it plainly, don't apologise.*

> I'll be straight with you: it's still a work in progress. I'm building it as I go, and there's gaps I'm still filling.

### 7 · Close

*2:10–2:25 · Let the last line sit. Don't talk over the end.*

> The election is Saturday the 7th of November. It'll be my first one.
>
> Have a look before then, and go in knowing what you're voting for.
>
> The link's in my bio, on Instagram and Facebook. It's politika.nz.
>
> This is for us whanau. Ngā mihi.

---

## Production notes

- **Two takes minimum of section 4.** It is the only part carrying new
  information, and it answers the two problems named in section 2.
- **Record the screen separately, after.** Your face for 1, 2, 3, 6, 7; the
  screen for 4 and 5. Do not try to demo live.
- **Checked against the live site:** 11 issues (`POLICY_TOPIC_ORDER`), election
  Saturday 7 November 2026 (`NEXT_ELECTION_DATE`). If either changes, the
  numbers in section 4 and the date in section 7 are the ones to re-check.
- **About 350 words, roughly 2:20.** Section 2 is the longest block and the most
  personal. If the whole thing feels rushed, take the time there rather than
  trimming it.

---

## Still open

- **The *pono* line.** The original recording ended on something that
  transcribed as "that's why I've built this outa Pono". If those are te reo
  words about truth, they are a stronger close than "This one's for us".
- **The first-vote bookend.** Section 1 says he has never voted and nothing
  picks it back up. One line in the close, such as "This'll be my first vote",
  would close the loop.
- **A 60-second cut** for Reels and TikTok, where section 4 is most of the video.

---

## Why it is shaped this way

Three things changed from the raw recording, and they are the reusable part:

1. **The comparison got the most time and a concrete walkthrough.** The original
   said the site helps people understand parties but never how, which is the one
   thing no political ad gives them.
2. **The two problems came first.** Once "I'd have to visit every party's site"
   and "I couldn't understand it" are named, the demo reads as an answer rather
   than a feature list.
3. **"Work in progress" moved to near the end.** Leading with it undercut
   everything after; after the demo it reads as honesty instead of an apology.

## Feedback on this draft

### What is working

- **"I'm thirty years old, and I've never voted. Not once."** The best thing in
  the script by a distance. It is rare, it takes something to say publicly, and
  it means you are not lecturing anyone. Every viewer who has never voted has
  just found the one person talking about this who is not above them.
- **The two problems.** "You've got to go to their website, every party, one at
  a time" is concrete and true. Most sites in this space describe a vague
  problem. You describe a task you actually failed at.
- **"If we don't have a party's position on something yet, the page says so."**
  Quietly the most trust-building line in the script, and it costs three seconds.

### Applied

- **The close now returns to the opening.** "It'll be my first one" turns the
  video into a loop instead of a list. Before, the never-voted line was handed
  to the audience in the first fifteen seconds and then dropped.
- **Section 3 cut to "It's called Politika, and it's free."** It was defining
  the site, then section 4 defined it again with more detail.

### Your call

- **Section 5 is a shrug.** "There's other tools in there too, have a look
  around" costs ten seconds and gives nobody a reason to do anything. Either cut
  it, which loses nothing, or name one thing. The bills tracker is the strongest
  candidate: "what they've actually done, not what they say" is a different
  promise from the rest of the video.
- **There is no ask.** "Have a look" is weak for a launch video. Two stronger
  options, pick one, not both: "go find the issue you care about most and start
  there" is better for the viewer; "send it to someone who's not voting" is
  better for turnout, which is the point of the site.
- **"Work in progress" is the one moment that gives someone a reason not to
  bother.** It is honest, and everything else in the script earns trust by being
  specific rather than by apologising. If it stays, make it forward-facing: "I'm
  still adding to it" rather than "there's gaps I'm still filling". Same fact,
  opposite feeling.

### One risk

The date locks the video to this election. The original recording said this
should be here for the next ones too, and as written section 7 is unusable after
7 November. Record an alternative take of that block alone, "there's an election
coming" instead of the date, and there is an evergreen cut of the same video.
